Best Porn Blocker for iPhone: What Actually Works in 2026

September 14, 2026 · 4 min read

If you've searched for a porn blocker for iPhone, you've found a hundred options and no way to tell which ones actually work. Most don't. Here's why, what to look for, and how we built Relustt to handle it.

Why most iPhone porn blockers fail

There are three ways a blocker fails you, and almost every app on the market fails in at least one.

1. It's trivial to turn off. You install it, feel good, and the first time a real urge hits at 1am you open Settings, flip a toggle, and it's gone. A blocker that can be disabled in ten seconds is a speed bump, not a wall. Urges last minutes. Ten seconds is nothing.

2. It only blocks the obvious stuff. It catches the big tube sites and nothing else. Image searches sail through. The social feed that gets you warmed up sails through. Reddit sails through. You end up at the same place by a slightly longer road.

3. It routes your traffic through someone's server. A lot of "blockers" are actually VPNs. Every site you visit, every app you open, goes through their infrastructure. That's how they filter it. It also means a company you've never heard of has a complete log of your online life. For a product whose whole purpose is privacy and shame reduction, that's a bad trade.

What a blocker actually needs

Here's the checklist. If an app misses two of these, keep looking.

  • On-device filtering. Blocks locally on your iPhone using iOS's native content blocking. No proxy, no VPN, no traffic to anyone's server. Your browsing never leaves your phone.
  • Covers more than websites. Adult sites, obviously. But also image searches, and the app feeds that reliably lead you there.
  • Friction to disable. A delay, an accountability step, something that makes turning it off take longer than the urge lasts. This is the single most important feature and the one most apps skip.
  • Something for the actual moment. Blocking removes the easy path. It doesn't remove the urge. A blocker that also gives you something to do in the 90 seconds when it hits is doing twice the job.
  • Tracks the streak. Sounds minor. Isn't. Seeing "Day 23" on a screen does something to your commitment that a vague sense of "it's been a while" doesn't.

What about Screen Time?

Apple's built-in Screen Time has a "Limit Adult Websites" toggle. It's free and it's better than nothing.

Its problems: it only filters Safari and some browsers, it misses most image search and in-app content, and if you know your own Screen Time passcode you can disable it in seconds. A trick some guys use is having a friend or partner set the passcode. That helps, but it's still a website filter, not a recovery tool.

Use it as a first layer if you want. Don't mistake it for the solution.

The honest truth about bypassing

Any blocker can be bypassed. You can delete the app. You can use a different device. If someone's determined enough, no software stops them.

That's not the point. The point is that most relapses aren't determined. They're a tired brain at 1am taking the path of least resistance. Make that path five minutes longer, and most of the time the urge dies before you finish. The blocker doesn't need to be unbreakable. It needs to be slower than the urge.

Common questions

Does Screen Time block porn on iPhone?+

Partially. Screen Time's 'Limit Adult Websites' filter catches a lot of obvious sites, but it's easy to disable if you know your own passcode, it misses most image searches and app feeds, and it doesn't do anything for the moments when you actually need help. It's a decent first layer, not a solution.

Can a porn blocker be bypassed on iPhone?+

Any blocker can be bypassed by someone determined enough, including by deleting the app. The goal isn't an unbreakable wall. It's making the bypass take longer than the urge lasts, which for most urges is under five minutes. Delay and friction are what make a blocker effective.

Is an on-device porn blocker safer than a VPN-based one?+

Yes. VPN-style blockers route all your traffic through the provider's servers, which means they can see everything you do online. An on-device content blocker filters locally, on your phone, and nothing leaves the device. Same blocking, no privacy trade-off.
